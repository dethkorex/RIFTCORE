# Riftcore campaign level design review

The campaign has ten sectors and twenty acts. Act I leads from deployment through two gate relays to the miniboss and extraction. Act II has its own pair of relays, main boss and extraction. Layout changes use fixed Godot geometry. The two Abyssal Plant acts use their existing authored deck lists. Map lengths, arenas, checkpoint identities and extraction markers were preserved.

Build traversal around the machinery and hazards established for each sector. Ember Foundry uses casting lines, quench drains, maintenance gantries and freight lifts. Keep boats and ferries out of campaign designs.

## Movement and spacing

A Godot 4.7.2 trial at 60 physics ticks per second used the actual operator on a flat deck. At full running speed (9 m/s), holding jump gave a **2.90 m rise** and **7.65 m of horizontal travel** in 0.85 seconds. Dashing near the apex gave **10.32 m** in 0.95 seconds. These are centre-to-centre measurements with room to accelerate. Elevated landings, short run-ups, ice, wind, water currents and low ceilings change the usable distance. The ordinary jump has a 12.5 m/s launch, 28 m/s² ascending gravity and stronger descending gravity. The standing capsule is 1.70 m tall and 0.84 m wide. Wall kicks launch at 6.7 m/s sideways and 13.5 m/s upward.

Three design decisions follow directly from the trials.

- At 32 fork entrances, a low shelf left about 14 cm above a standing operator. Raising the top by 22 cm and thinning the slab increased the opening to about 2.24 m. The upper path still starts with an ordinary jump around the front edge. Both the headroom and the jump were checked at every affected fork.
- The first landing above a shaft must catch the horizontal speed of a wall kick. Separate jumps between platforms passed in BioLab and Scrapyard before a continuous shaft test exposed an overshoot. The receiving decks are now 7.6 m wide. The precision jump happens *after* the player lands.
- Every locked boss door identifies a landmark for the next missing relay. Interacting returns the operator to the entrance of that route. The return preserves the relay count and the player still has to complete the climb, crossing or course. The rocket-board return goes to the boarding checkpoint.

## Collision and progression checks

- Platform edits moved or scaled the visual kit and its box collider together. The operator remains on the existing side-scrolling plane. Phase decks stay visible while solid. Two lamps distinguish the warning, absent, and returning phases. The Scrapyard press's damage shape follows the moving jaw and teeth.
- The scene tests loaded each act through the game. They checked enabled collision shapes, grounded deployment, both checkpoint floors, relay interaction, landmark prompts, return landings, and the revised jumps. The rocket board awards its relay through the course-specific test.
- The campaign progression suite exercised all ten miniboss and main-boss transitions, extraction and rewards. It places the operator at objectives and accelerates boss defeats. Combat difficulty is outside its scope.
- Separate controller trials exercised 21 wall-kick shaft exits, 96 loft checks across 16 acts, and the sump switchbacks and shutters across those acts. The Abyssal suite covered actual jumps, the flooded conduit, valves, wall kicking and the electric safe window. Foundry lava contact, Frozen Reactor board behavior, jungle progression and crouch/drop controls also passed.
- All 20 acts passed a placement scan for supported ground enemies and chests, excessively narrow enemy landings by the scan's criteria, and sampled firing lanes to shootable devices. The scan excludes flying enemies. It verifies placement at spawn, not every later AI position.
- Dedicated hazard tests sampled phase-deck ray collisions and visibility at five points in the cycle, then checked a real operator above, against and clear of the crusher jaw.

## What still needs a human run

The automated trials run in Godot. They reposition the operator between set pieces and often freeze enemies. They do **not** constitute an uninterrupted solo campaign or a live four-player session. Camera framing, blind drops on a first visit, difficulty under combat pressure, party catch-up after a gate return and whether optional rewards are worth the detour still need playtesting. Some short lower passages deliberately retain crouch clearances. The 32 fork fixes do not imply every overhead passage was widened.

The two additional regression suites in the project are `tests/level_landings.gd` and `tests/terrain_hazard_alignment.gd`. Run each from the project directory with `godot --headless --path . --fixed-fps 60 --script res://tests/<filename>`.
